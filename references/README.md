# References

- `keel.json`: the bibliography, in CSL-JSON. One entry per work or dataset cited on the site. Keys are the first author's surname (or one word of an organization's name, or its initials) plus the year, with a letter when two works would share an in-text citation (`kulveit2025`, `herdagdelen2026a`, `bls2026`); a work with no author uses consecutive words of its title (`stackoverflow2023`); an executive order uses `eo` and its number (`eo14434`); datasets use descriptive keys (`bls_labor_share`). Entries of type `dataset` form the "Data sources" section of the Sources page; all others form "References". Each entry is verified against its original before it is added (`review/sources-verification.md`).
- `chicago-author-date.csl`: the official Citation Style Language style for the Chicago Manual of Style, 18th edition (2024), author-date ("Chicago Manual of Style 18th edition (author-date)"), downloaded on 2026-11-18 from https://raw.githubusercontent.com/citation-style-language/styles/1aaf551f864d50e875a617b4faf2bf5da30f3f15/chicago-author-date.csl (styles repository commit 1aaf551f864d50e875a617b4faf2bf5da30f3f15, 2026-04-14, the latest commit touching the file on that date; SHA-256 002fade78d7e4fe9d42936a16b43a8066b097013f6255df40b1bfba6631eff9b). It replaces the 17th-edition file used in Part 1 (owner decision, 2026-11-18). It is the reference against which `indicators.bibliography` was checked (by rendering `keel.json` with it through Quarto's Pandoc), and it can be used for in-text citations through Quarto.

The Sources page (`site/sources.qmd`, generated part `site/_generated/sources.md`) is rendered by `indicators.bibliography`, which applies the rules below directly so that every entry gets an anchor equal to its key and the two sections stay separate. Run `python -m indicators.pages` to rebuild it, and `python -m indicators.bibliography check-links` to check every URL and DOI (writes `review/sources-link-check.md`). The page itself does not name the style (owner request, 2026-11-18).

## Chicago 18th-edition rules applied

Sources: the CSL style above; Monash University Library, "Chicago 18th (B) Author-Date: Journal and magazine articles" (https://guides.lib.monash.edu/chicago-18-b-author-date/journal-articles); Curtin University Library, "Chicago 18th Author-Date: Websites & social media" (https://uniskills.library.curtin.edu.au/referencing/chicago18/websites-and-social-media); Massey University, "Author-date reference list in Chicago style" (18th edition; https://www.massey.ac.nz/study/study-and-assignment-support-and-guides/academic-writing/referencing/chicago-manual-of-style-referencing-style/chicago-manual-of-style-reference-list/). All read on 2026-11-18. The Chicago Manual of Style Online requires a login and was not read.

| Rule | 17th edition (Part 1) | 18th edition (applied) | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authors in the reference list | up to ten listed; more than ten, first seven and "et al." | up to six listed; seven or more, first three and "et al." | CSL `<bibliography et-al-min="7" et-al-use-first="3">`; Monash ("Name up to six authors in the reference list"; more than six, the first three and "et al."); Massey ("more than 6 authors ... only list the first 3 authors ... followed by 'et al.'") |
| Authors in in-text citations | four or more: "et al." | three or more: first author and "et al." ("Chooi et al. 2026") | CSL `<citation et-al-min="3" et-al-use-first="1">`; Monash ("more than two authors ... only the first author, followed by 'et al.'") |
| Month and day of web pages, blog posts and preprints | full date with the year repeated ("October 27, 2026.") | month and day after the container, year not repeated ("*Discover* (blog), March 3.") | Curtin ("The blog month and day follow the title without repeating the year"; example "Huberman, Ben. 2016. ... *Discover* (blog), March 3."); Monash preprint example ("Preprint, PsyArXiv, September 4."); the CSL style's output ("Ghosts of Electricity, September 29.") |
| Preprints | "Preprint, arXiv:2501.16946, January 28, 2025." | "Preprint, arXiv, January 28." (the DOI carries the identifier) | Monash: "Author Surname, First Name. Year. 'Title of Article.' Preprint, preprint server name, Month DD. DOI or URL." |
| Blog name | italic, "(blog)" unless the name says so, then a period | italic, "(blog)" unless the name says so, then a comma and the publisher and date ("*Diff* (blog), Wikimedia Foundation, October 17.") | Curtin example; CSL output ("Diff, Wikimedia Foundation, October 17.") |
| Place of publication | given ("Santa Monica, CA: RAND Corporation") | omitted; recorded in `keel.json` but not shown | Massey ("The publisher location is no longer required in the 18th edition"); CSL (the `publisher-place` line is commented out) |
| Proceedings editors | not shown | "edited by" with the same six/three rule ("edited by Mubashara Akhtar, Jan Batzner, Leshem Choshen, et al.") | CSL (editors use the bibliography's et-al settings) |
| Executive orders | (none cited) | legal form: "Exec. Order No. 14434, 91 Fed. Reg. 63129 (2026)." | CSL output for a `legislation` item (the style follows Bluebook for legal citations; its short title reads "CMOS/CMS with Bluebook (author-date/AD [13.102])") |
| Same in-text citation | letters for the same authors and year | letters whenever two works would share an in-text citation, including "et al." groups with different coauthors (`massenkoff2026a`, `massenkoff2026b`) | CMOS letter-suffix practice; the CSL style instead adds names until citations differ, which gives long citations; letters chosen for readability (KEEL choice) |

Kept from Part 1: datasets carry "n.d.", the access date and the license; titles in headline style; standalone works in italics, parts in quotation marks; page ranges condensed (CMOS 9.64); a DOI, when present, is the link shown; repeated authors written out; names as printed on the work (owner decision). Dates with a month but no day (for example a report dated "August 2026") show the year only. US-government author names keep the "U.S." printed by the agency (names as printed).

In-text citations on the pages link to the entry's anchor on the Sources page: parenthetical "[(Kulveit et al. 2025)](sources.qmd#kulveit2025)", narrative "[Kulveit et al. (2025)](sources.qmd#kulveit2025) argue", with a locator for quotations and specific parts ("(Chooi et al. 2026, 232)", "(Handa et al. 2025, app. E)"). Data sources are linked by name to their entry (for example "[FRED](sources.qmd#fred_gdp)"). Generated text (summary page, indicator summary tables and Method sections) uses `bibliography.in_text`.
