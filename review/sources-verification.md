# Sources verification

Every entry in `references/keel.json` was checked against its original on 2026-11-18 before it was added: the landing page, PDF or DOI record was opened (scripted download with text extraction, or a web fetch where the site blocks scripts) and the fields below were compared. Nothing was entered from memory. Titles are given in Chicago headline-style capitalization; where the original uses sentence case, that is the only change.

Chicago date placement (owner question; updated 2026-11-18 for the 18th edition, owner decision): the year follows the authors, and where the source gives a month and day the month and day follow the container without the year ("2026. ... Anthropic Research, October 27."). Sources: Curtin University Library, "Chicago 18th Author-Date: Websites & social media" (https://uniskills.library.curtin.edu.au/referencing/chicago18/websites-and-social-media): "The blog month and day follow the title without repeating the year", example "Huberman, Ben. 2016. ... Discover (blog), March 3."; Monash University Library, "Chicago 18th (B) Author-Date" preprint example "Preprint, PsyArXiv, September 4." (https://guides.lib.monash.edu/chicago-18-b-author-date/journal-articles); and the official CSL chicago-author-date style (18th ed.), whose output agrees. (The University of Queensland's 18th-edition blog example repeats the year; the majority form is used.) Applied to preprints, web pages, blog posts and reports whose source gives a month and day; proceedings papers and chapters carry the year only, and a month without a day is not shown. The Part 1 rule (17th edition, year repeated) is superseded. All 18th-edition rules applied are listed, with sources, in references/README.md.
