## Why this matters for gradual disempowerment

Gradual disempowerment is the scenario in which economies, states and cultures stop needing people and so stop serving them, without any single dramatic event (Kulveit et al., 2025). Culture, in this argument, stays tied to people because people make it and pass it on. Public knowledge commons are one place this happens in the open. On Stack Overflow, people ask questions and other people answer them. On Wikipedia, people write and read a shared reference.

If people turn to AI systems in place of these communities, two things follow:

- **Less human-made knowledge.** Fewer new questions, answers and articles are produced by people.
- **Thinner communities.** The communities that maintain and correct that knowledge shrink.

AI systems also learn from these commons, so a smaller commons could leave AI-generated material with a larger role in what people learn.

This indicator sits in the **Displacement** row for the cultural domain. It is a **leading** indicator, one that moves early: activity on these sites responds within months to changes in where people go for answers.

## What it shows

**Stack Overflow against comparison sites (drives the reading).** New questions on Stack Overflow, compared with new questions on four non-programming Stack Exchange sites, the **comparison sites**:

- **Home Improvement:** questions about home repair, renovation and maintenance, from plumbing and wiring to carpentry.
- **Travel:** questions about planning trips and getting around, such as visas, routes, lodging and local customs.
- **Role-playing Games:** questions about tabletop role-playing games, including rules, game design and running a group.
- **Science Fiction & Fantasy:** questions about science fiction and fantasy books, films and shows, including plot, canon and interpretation.

**The comparison sites were chosen in advance.** The author approved them on 17 November 2026, under criteria fixed before any post-2019 counts were requested or viewed:

- **Launched before 2015**, to give a long history before generative AI.
- **At least 400 new questions a month in 2019**, so monthly counts are not too noisy.
- **Subject matter outside programming, IT, data and math**, whose users are directly exposed to coding assistants.
- **Mostly experiential, physical-world, creative or community-specific subject matter**, where a text chatbot is a weaker substitute.
- **A "normal", active main site.** "Normal" is Stack Exchange's own term for a site that has left beta. A main site is a question-and-answer site, not a meta site for discussing the site itself.

The author decided to use only these four sites. The full selection record, including the sites considered and rejected, is in the project repository.

**The relative-activity index (the main measure).** Each month, KEEL adds up Stack Overflow's new questions over the past 12 months and divides that total by the geometric mean of the same 12-month totals for the four comparison sites. The result is scaled so that its 2019 average is 100. An index of 50 would mean Stack Overflow's activity, relative to the comparison sites, is half what it was in 2019. Using 12-month totals removes the seasonal pattern in these sites. Dividing by the comparison sites removes changes that hit all Stack Exchange sites alike, such as network-wide policy changes or a general shift away from question-and-answer sites. The geometric mean gives each comparison site equal weight, whatever its size.

This is a **relative-activity index, not a causal estimate**. Stack Overflow was already falling behind the comparison sites before ChatGPT, so the index does not isolate the effect of AI.

- **Change since November 2022.** The second headline figure is the percent change in the index since the 12 months ending November 2022, the last window almost entirely before ChatGPT's release.
- **Leave-one-out band.** The index is recomputed four times, each time dropping one comparison site and using the other three. The shaded band on the chart shows the lowest and highest of these four results. A narrow band means the result does not depend on any single site.
- **Year-over-year gap (alternative view).** Stack Overflow's year-over-year percent change in monthly questions, minus the average year-over-year change across the comparison sites. This was KEEL's first measure. It is kept as a second way to look at the same comparison.

**Stack Overflow's decline began before ChatGPT.** The number of surviving questions per year peaked in the mid-2010s and fell through the late 2010s. After a rise in 2020, the fall steepened in 2021 and 2022, before ChatGPT's release. The chart shows this full history.

**"Before AI" is not a clean line for programmers.** GitHub Copilot, an AI coding assistant, was announced as a technical preview on 29 June 2021 and became generally available on 21 June 2022. Programmers had AI help well before ChatGPT, so part of the 2021 and 2022 fall could already be AI-related.

**Chart markers.** The chart marks these dates without splitting the line:

- 29 June 2021: GitHub Copilot technical preview announced
- 21 June 2022: GitHub Copilot generally available
- 30 November 2022: ChatGPT released to the public
- 5 December 2022: Stack Overflow bans AI-generated answers
- 5 June 2023: Stack Overflow moderators begin a strike over the site's policy on AI-generated content

**The baseline.** This is a long-running series with a comparison group. Its baseline is how the index behaved in 2010 to 2019. The index starts in June 2012, the first month in which all four comparison sites have a full 12 months of questions, so its baseline covers June 2012 to December 2019. Readings start once the rules are fixed (see the methodology page). Until then the reading shows "Not yet reviewed."

**Wikipedia (shown alongside).** Two context series:

- **English Wikipedia human pageviews per month**, from July 2015. Wikimedia's own classification of human traffic, which excludes known crawlers and detected automated traffic.
- **English Wikipedia active editors per month.** Logged-in editors with five or more edits in the month, leaving out accounts that Wikimedia counts as bots (accounts in its bot group, or with "bot" in the user name).

These are shown for context and do not drive the reading.

## What it doesn't show

- **It does not measure human capacity or reliance on AI.** It counts activity: questions asked, pages viewed, editors editing. People may know as much as before and simply ask in a different place. KEEL names this gap on the methodology page.
- **It does not give the full AI effect.** AI affects the comparison sites too. People can ask a chatbot about wiring a light switch or a rule in a role-playing game. The index therefore measures Stack Overflow's change relative to sites that are less exposed. If AI has also reduced activity on the comparison sites, the index understates the effect on Stack Overflow.
- **It does not separate AI from other changes specific to Stack Overflow.** Moderation policy, community norms, the maturing of the site (many common questions are already answered) and changes in the programming tools people use can all move Stack Overflow on its own. The **AI link** is **moderate**:
  - The comparison sites hold changes across the whole Stack Exchange network roughly constant.
  - The decline that began before ChatGPT and the causes specific to Stack Overflow are only partly addressed.

  (A "strong" AI link would mean the series measures AI directly. It would not mean strong evidence of harm.)

## What would change our reading

### Warning sign

Stack Overflow's activity falling further behind the comparison sites than in its 2012 to 2019 history would be consistent with people turning from human knowledge communities to AI for coding help.

### Alternative explanations

- **For Stack Overflow, a decline already under way.** Stack Overflow's long decline, which began years before ChatGPT, may simply be continuing. That is why the reading rests on the comparison with other sites and on whether the index's path changed, not on the fall alone.
- **For Stack Overflow, changes specific to the site.** These would make the index overstate an AI-related change. Moderation and policy changes, the 2023 moderator strike, the site's maturing (many common questions are already answered) and new non-AI places and tools for programmers' questions can all reduce Stack Overflow's activity without touching the comparison sites. The index would count all of this as Stack Overflow falling behind.
- **For Stack Overflow, the line is blurred at both ends.** AI coding tools were spreading before ChatGPT, and AI also reaches the comparison sites. Both would tend to make the index understate an AI effect.
- **For Wikipedia, people may read it through AI.** Falling human pageviews may mean that people read Wikipedia's content through chatbots and search summaries that draw on it, instead of visiting the site. Fewer pageviews would then mean fewer visits, with the same reliance on human-written knowledge.

### What would raise or lower our confidence

**What would raise our confidence that a change is AI-related:**

- The index falling faster after late 2022 than its 2012 to 2019 path, by more than its usual variation.
- The same pattern across the leave-one-out band, so it does not depend on one comparison site.

**What would lower it:**

- A gap that depends on one comparison site.
- A gap that opened before late 2022 and kept the same pace afterward.
- A gap that tracks a Stack Overflow policy change.

**Planned for Stage 2, when the rules are fixed:**

- A "normal divergence" range, meaning how far two sites' activity usually drifts apart for reasons that have nothing to do with AI. It would come from placebo comparisons: the same index computed for sites where AI is not expected to matter much.
- Thresholds applied to calendar-year values of the index, rather than to single months.
- A test of whether the index's trend changed after late 2022.

**Planned later:** a study of the index month by month around each marked event, a wider pool of comparison sites, and a correction for deleted questions (see Caveats).

These indicators show whether the conditions for disempowerment are present. They do not show whether it is happening.

## Data and calculation

- **Source and series.** Stack Exchange API: the number of new questions created in each calendar month on Stack Overflow and on the four comparison sites (Home Improvement, Travel, Role-playing Games, and Science Fiction & Fantasy). Wikimedia Analytics API: monthly English Wikipedia pageviews by human users, and monthly counts of logged-in editors with five or more edits, leaving out accounts Wikimedia counts as bots.
- **Units.** Index (2019 average = 100); percent for the change since November 2022; percentage points for the year-over-year gap; questions, pageviews and editors per month for the underlying counts.
- **Frequency.** Monthly. The current month is left out until it is complete.
- **Date range.** Stack Overflow counts from September 2008. Each comparison site starts at its first full month after its public beta. The index starts in June 2012 and the year-over-year gap in July 2012. Wikipedia pageviews start in July 2015; active editors in January 2010.
- **Formula in plain words.**
  - Index: for each month, Stack Overflow's questions over the 12 months ending that month, divided by the geometric mean of the four comparison sites' 12-month totals. The result is divided by its 2019 average (a geometric average) and multiplied by 100.
  - Change since November 2022: the index this month divided by the index for the 12 months ending November 2022, minus 1, times 100.
  - Leave-one-out band: the index computed four more times, each time with one comparison site left out; the band runs from the lowest to the highest of the four.
  - Year-over-year gap: Stack Overflow's percent change from the same month a year earlier, minus the average of the four comparison sites' percent changes.

## Caveats

- **Wikipedia's improved bot detection.** In 2025 Wikimedia improved its detection of automated traffic that had been counted as human, and used the new method to reclassify its data from March 2025. Earlier months were not reprocessed, so the improved classification exists only from March 2025, and KEEL cannot use it for the years before. After the revision, Wikimedia reported that human pageviews across all language versions of Wikipedia had fallen roughly 8 percent compared with the same months of 2024 ([Wikimedia, 17 October 2025](https://diff.wikimedia.org/2025/10/17/new-user-trends-on-wikipedia/)). Some of the drop in the chart across March 2025 reflects better bot detection, so the chart marks a break there.
- **Deleted questions are not counted.** Question counts come from the Stack Exchange API as of the download date, so questions deleted since they were asked are missing. Deletion rates differ by site and accumulate with age, so older months are undercounted. Its size is unknown; it likely makes the index understate Stack Overflow's decline. Every download is archived, so the build-up of deletions can be measured later.
- **Small sites are noisier.** Each comparison site gets far fewer questions a month than Stack Overflow, so their counts jump around more. Using 12-month totals and four sites reduces this.
- **The retrospective is not yet computed.** The retrospective applies the fixed rule to every quarter from the fourth quarter of 2022 onward, to show how readings would have changed since ChatGPT's release. It will be computed once and published as a dated snapshot in Stage 3.
