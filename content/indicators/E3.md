## Why this matters for gradual disempowerment

The gradual disempowerment argument turns on decisions and tasks moving from people to AI. Kulveit et al. (2025) list "the fraction of decisions made by AI" among the metrics worth developing. No public data measures that across society.

The Anthropic Economic Index is the nearest public proxy. It classifies conversations with Claude by how people work with the AI:

- **Automation**: the AI is asked to complete a task with little involvement from the person. Anthropic's "directive" and "feedback loop" patterns.
- **Augmentation**: the person and the AI work on it together. Anthropic's "validation," "task iteration" and "learning" patterns.

This indicator sits in the **exposure** row as a measure of the depth of exposure. E2 asks whether firms use AI at all. E3 asks, for one product, how much of the work is handed over. It is a **leading** indicator.

**Disclosure.** This data is published by Anthropic, and this site is built with assistance from Claude. Readers should weigh the indicator with that in mind.

## What it shows

- **The automation share and the augmentation share of classified Claude.ai conversations**, worldwide, one point per release.
- **The data come from the Anthropic Economic Index releases on Hugging Face.** These appear at irregular intervals. Each release is harmonized to the same two shares, and every change in how a release is built is recorded as a series break (below).

**The denominator.** Some conversations do not fit any of the five patterns, and Anthropic labels them "none" or "not classified." The site leaves those out, so the automation and augmentation shares always add up to 100. Some of Anthropic's report headlines count every conversation in the denominator, including those that fit no pattern. For example, the September 2025 report puts automation at about 49 percent of Claude.ai conversations. On the site's classified-only basis, the same release gives 51.1 percent. Both are correct for their own denominators. The site uses the classified-only version so that a change in how many conversations go unclassified does not move the series.

**Series breaks.** Each is annotated on the chart.

- **15 September 2025:** the release layout changed, and shares are recomputed from the underlying patterns.
- **24 March 2026:** the Claude.ai population widened from Free and Pro plans to Free, Pro and Max plans.
- **1 April 2026:** releases moved to calendar-month aggregates with published automation and augmentation groupings, and the Claude.ai data began to include Cowork, an agentic product.

The comparison is **AI-native**. The baseline is the series' own recent path, at the frequency the releases allow. Readings use the exposure scale (Low, Rising, Pervasive) and start in Stage 2. Until then the reading shows "Not yet reviewed."

## What it doesn't show

- **It does not show AI use across society.** This is **one vendor, one product**: conversations on Claude.ai. Claude accounts for a minority of AI use, and its users are not a representative sample of people who use AI. Use through Anthropic's API and through other companies' products is not included.
- **It does not show disempowerment.** The AI-specificity badge is **strong** because the series measures AI use directly. It does not mean the series shows lost leverage. A task handed to AI may free a person for work they value more.
- **It does not measure decisions.** Automation here means a task completed with little back-and-forth in a conversation. It does not show who made the decision the task served, or whether a person reviewed the result.
- **It has no pre-AI baseline.** The series begins in early 2025. There is no earlier period to compare it with, so timing cannot serve as evidence here.
- **It does not measure welfare.** People who automate more of their tasks may be better off, worse off, or neither. This series tracks how work is shared with AI. It says nothing about who gains from that.

## What would change our reading

**Direction assumption.** A rising automation share would be consistent with decisions and tasks moving from people to AI.

**Main rival reading.** A rising automation share may reflect Anthropic's own product changes rather than society delegating more. Agentic tools such as Cowork are built to complete tasks with less back-and-forth, so adding them to the data, or more people adopting them, can raise the automation share without any change in how willing people are to hand over work. Changes in who uses Claude, such as the March 2026 plan widening, can do the same.

**What would raise our confidence that a change reflects wider delegation:**

- A rise in automation that holds within a stable population and product mix, between releases with no break.
- A similar rise in usage data from other AI providers, if any is published.

**What would lower it:**

- A rise that appears only at a product launch or a change in coverage.
- A rise that reverses in the next release.

Like every indicator on this site, this one shows conditions under which the hypothesized mechanism would operate. It does not prove that disempowerment is happening.

## Caveats

- **Conflict of interest.** Anthropic publishes the data and has a commercial interest in how its products are perceived. The site is built with assistance from Claude. The site uses the published data as released and documents every choice it makes in harmonizing it.
- **Irregular releases.** Releases arrive months apart, and the gaps between them differ. Readings compare releases, whatever the time between them.
- **Changing schemas.** Each release has been structured differently. The mapping from each release's files to the two shares is recorded in the series notes, and any future layout change will be added as a break.
- **Classification by model.** Conversations are classified automatically by Anthropic's own methods. A change in the classifier could move the shares. The breaks list records only the changes the releases document.
