# Annotated Bibliography: Indicators of Gradual Disempowerment

*Compiled 2026-11-12; additions from an independent review, 2026-11-12.*

This is a list of every source used in the research behind the Disempowerment Indicators site. The site tracks the "gradual disempowerment" scenario described by Kulveit et al. (2025). Under that scenario, economies, states and cultures come to depend less on human participation, and people lose the leverage that participation gave them. It is a public source record for site visitors and researchers. Entries say what each source is, what the project used it for, and any caveats the research found.

## Legend

**Type:** Paper · Book · Report · Dataset · Survey · Blog or essay · News or secondary · Tool or docs

**Used for:** the v1 indicator(s) a source supports, or its other role:

| ID | Indicator |
|---|---|
| E1 | Labor share of income (profit share as mirror) |
| E2 | Firm AI adoption (Census BTOS) |
| E3 | How AI is used: automation vs. augmentation (Anthropic Economic Index) |
| E4 | Wealth concentration (top 1% / 0.1%) |
| G1 | Labor-based share of tax revenue |
| G2 | Federal AI use cases |
| G3 | Lobbying by AI companies |
| C1 | Human knowledge-commons activity (Stack Overflow, Wikipedia) |
| C2 | AI share of cultural production vs. consumption |

Other roles: **counter-evidence panel** (signals pointing away from disempowerment), **framework** (the site's concepts and structure), **method** (how to measure or interpret), **background only** (context, critiques, or candidates for later versions; "later version" means the design lists it for a future release), **oversight gap** (evidence on humans deferring to AI, for which no public time series exists yet).


**Link checks:** every URL was requested on 2026-11-12 with an automated client. Links that did not return a success or redirect code are marked "⚠ link not confirmed" with the HTTP code. Many sites block automated requests (403, 429) or drop the connection, so a warning often means a human browser would still reach the page. It does not mean the link is wrong. URLs were not changed.

**Verification status:** many figures in the research came from search summaries or press coverage, not the primary source. The design rule is that no secondary-only figure goes on the site. Annotations below say when a source was only partly read.

## A. The gradual disempowerment framework and follow-up work


AI Objectives Institute (n.d.). **Gradual Disempowerment Observatory.** Project page. [ai.objectives.institute](https://ai.objectives.institute/gradual-disempowerment-observatory)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
Describes a planned observatory with compound indices of human agency across the economy, politics and culture, threshold-detection models, and a "continuously updated dashboard." The research could not find any published data or metric values. It is the closest announced effort to this site.

Chooi, Lee & Li (2026). **Measuring AI-Induced Disempowerment: A Framework and Proposed Metrics.** EvalEval Workshop (ACL), pp. 227–236, DOI 10.18653/v1/2026.evaleval-1.36. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.evaleval-1.36/)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** framework; method\
Uses Sen's model of agency and a three-layer model (exposure → erosion → lock-in) across economic, political and cultural domains. It finds that existing measurement "clusters almost entirely at exposure." It proposes six metrics, including centaur evaluations, disempowerment perception surveys, and monitoring capital flows to and from human labor. The site's grid rows come from this paper. **Only the abstract was read.** The metric definitions and the actors suggested for each were not seen.

Christiano, P. (2019). **What failure looks like.** AI Alignment Forum. [alignmentforum.org](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/HBxe6wdjxK239zajf/what-failure-looks-like)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** background only\
A short essay often read as the precursor to gradual disempowerment. Part I ("going out with a whimper") describes society optimizing for easily measured proxies until human understanding and control quietly slip away.

Critch, A. & Russell, S. (2023). **TASRA: a Taxonomy and Analysis of Societal-Scale Risks from AI.** arXiv 2306.06924. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06924)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
A taxonomy of societal-scale AI risks that places diffuse, systemic failures (no single bad actor) alongside misalignment and misuse.

Davidson, T., Finnveden, L. & Hadshar, R. (2025). **AI-Enabled Coups: How a Small Group Could Use AI to Seize Power.** Forethought. [forethought.org](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
The fast, deliberate counterpart to gradual disempowerment: how AI could let a small group take power. Useful for thinking about how gradual and abrupt risks interact.

Douglas, R. (2025). **Gradual Disempowerment: Concrete Research Projects.** Alignment Forum, 29 May 2025. [alignmentforum.org](https://alignmentforum.org/posts/GAv4DRGyDHe2orvwB/gradual-disempowerment-concrete-research-projects)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** method; framework\
A coauthor's agenda. Its "Indicators and Policy" section asks which indicators matter, how to build a representative set, what governments can track legibly (noting they may have incentives not to), and how to act on indicators "without Goodharting them." It cites the Anthropic Economic Index as an example measure. This is the main reason the site publishes its thresholds and freezes them before use.

Drago, L. & Laine, R. (2025). **The Intelligence Curse.** intelligence-curse.ai and Substack. [intelligence-curse.ai](https://intelligence-curse.ai/defining/) · [Substack](https://lukedrago.substack.com/p/the-intelligence-curse)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** G1; framework\
Argues by analogy to the "resource curse" that states whose revenue no longer depends on taxing workers may stop serving citizens. Its summary of rentier-state research also notes that the direction is ambiguous: rulers with non-labor revenue sometimes co-opt citizens with welfare rather than neglecting them. Sources give different origin dates (January vs. April 2025).

Duvenaud, D. (2025). **David Duvenaud on gradual disempowerment** (podcast interview, recorded 21 Aug 2025). 80,000 Hours Podcast. [80000hours.org](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/david-duvenaud-gradual-disempowerment/)\
**Type:** Blog or essay (podcast) · **Used for:** framework; method\
A coauthor calls the paper's core claims "very hard to operationalise" and says the employment rate "might not really matter, or be the right question to ask." He also mentions that coauthor Deger Turan (CEO of Metaculus) and others are building a "Gradual Disempowerment Index." The research found no public output from that index.

equilibria1 (author not identified) (n.d.). **Spectral Signatures of Gradual Disempowerment.** Substack. [equilibria1.substack.com](https://equilibria1.substack.com/p/spectral-signatures-of-gradual-disempowerment)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** background only\
An informal post proposing spectral graph measures (spectral gap, Fiedler vector) on influence networks. It was not read in full. Treat it as low-authority.

Gaventa, J. (1980). **Power and Powerlessness: Quiescence and Rebellion in an Appalachian Valley.** University of Illinois Press. (Book; no link.)\
**Type:** Book · **Used for:** framework\
A classic empirical study of power in a coal-mining valley, including how power shapes which grievances people raise at all. It informs what the site means by human "leverage" and why measured participation can overstate real influence.

Hendrycks, D. (2023). **Natural Selection Favors AIs over Humans.** arXiv 2303.16200. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16200)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
Argues that competitive pressure will select for AI systems that displace human control. A version of the competitive dynamics behind gradual disempowerment.

Herdağdelen, A., Joren, H. & Tamkin, A. (2026). **How AI agents shape experts' judgment calls.** Anthropic Research, 27 Oct 2026. [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** oversight gap\
A randomized study in which experts mostly followed an AI agent's randomly assigned lean, often repeating its arguments without crediting it. It frames the result as disempowerment, cites Kulveit et al., and proposes measuring deferral. It is the main evidence cited in the site's "human deferral / oversight" cell, which has no public time series yet. Published by Anthropic, which also publishes E3's data; figures should be taken from the full paper before citing.

Jedlicka, P. O. (2026). **Why do we need social singularity?** arXiv 2608.03904 (submitted 4 Aug 2026). [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03904)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** counter-evidence panel; framework\
Argues that gradual scenarios are "insufficiently sociologically specified," technologically deterministic, and underestimate collective agency and social conflict (mobilization, backlash) during AI diffusion. A search snippet says it engages Kulveit et al. directly; the abstract page read did not name them. It is the reason the site shows countervailing signals alongside erosion signals.

joepstorm (author and date not stated). **Tracking AI Influence** (dashboard). GitHub Pages. [joepstorm.github.io](https://joepstorm.github.io/gradual_disempowerment_dashboard/)\
**Type:** Tool or docs · **Used for:** background only\
An unattributed dashboard with Economic, Governmental, Cultural and News tabs. When fetched, its panels showed only "Loading...", so its metrics and sources could not be identified. Listed as a comparable project.

Krueger, D. (2025). **A few quick thoughts on measuring disempowerment.** LessWrong (via GreaterWrong mirror), 8 Dec 2025. [greaterwrong.com](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/hP2Pms2acPW6eeD6B/a-few-quick-thoughts-on-measuring-disempowerment)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** method; framework\
A coauthor warns that many metrics "don't separate empowering uses of AI from disempowering ones." Heavy AI use can look like progress while people lose real agency. He proposes pairing adoption data with interviews, testing whether human overseers just rubber-stamp AI output, and measuring how well the people nominally in charge can predict a system. A commenter notes that this misses institutional failure modes. It is the basis for the site's augmentation-vs-displacement note.

Kulveit, J., Douglas, R., Ammann, N., Turan, D., Krueger, D. & Duvenaud, D. (2025). **Gradual Disempowerment: Systemic Existential Risks from Incremental AI Development.** arXiv 2501.16946 (v2); ICML 2025 position paper. [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16946) · [HTML version](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.16946) · [project site](https://gradual-disempowerment.ai)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** framework (all indicators)\
The source paper. It argues that economies, states and cultures stay roughly aligned with people partly because they need human labor, taxes, compliance and attention. As AI replaces that participation, the pressure weakens, and the effects in each system reinforce one another. Sec. 6.2 lists metrics to develop (labor and AI share of GDP, AI share of consumed content, legislative complexity, AI in policy and security, and others) but gives no data sources, units or thresholds. The authors also concede that some outcomes are ambiguous, for example that an AI-run state "could provide some enormous boons." The research did not read the references and appendix (about the last 19k characters).

Lukes, S. (1974; 2nd ed. 2005). **Power: A Radical View.** Palgrave Macmillan. (Book; no link.)\
**Type:** Book · **Used for:** framework\
The "three dimensions of power" framework: power shows up in decisions, in what reaches the agenda, and in shaping what people want. It is relevant to the cultural leg of the argument and to why influence is hard to measure.

Manheim, D. & Garrabrant, S. (2018). **Categorizing Variants of Goodhart's Law.** arXiv 1803.04585. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** method\
Explains the ways a measure stops being informative once it becomes a target. Basis for the methodology page's statement that the indicators are diagnostics, not targets for policy.

Millidge, B. (2025). **Gradual Disempowerment Might Not Be So Bad.** beren.io, 23 Nov 2025. [beren.io](https://www.beren.io/2025-11-23-Gradual-Disempowerment-Might-Not-Be-So-Bad)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** framework; background only\
Argues that gradual disempowerment is "essentially the lived experience of almost all individual humans anyway." He suggests AI governance might be better than human governance, and that capital income and transfers could sustain living standards. He calls the outcome "highly uncertain." The key point for this project is that material welfare and influence can move in opposite directions. Every economic page carries a note on this.

Moon & Boudreaux (2026). **A Formal Model of How Artificial Intelligence Erodes Human Agency.** RAND Corporation, Research Report RR-A4817-1, April 2026. [rand.org](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4817-1.html) ⚠ link not confirmed (HTTP 403; the site may block automated requests)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
According to a search summary, it proposes tracking how many humans remain in the "decisive coalitions" that determine outcomes across domains, and benchmarks for when AI reduces that number or shapes which options humans see. **The page returned 403 to the research, so this is second-hand** (see the AIGL summary in Section G). Verify before citing.

Sahoo, S. (2026). **Policy myopia as a mechanism of gradual disempowerment in Post-AGI governance, Circa 2049.** arXiv 2603.03267, 3 Mar 2026. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03267)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
Recent work extending the gradual disempowerment framing to governance. Not yet read in full.

Sharma, M., McCain, M., Douglas, R., Duvenaud, D., et al. (2026). **Who's in Charge? Disempowerment Patterns in Real-World LLM Usage.** arXiv 2601.19062 (January 2026; reportedly ICML 2026). [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19062) · [Anthropic summary](https://www.anthropic.com/news/disempowerment-patterns)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
The only empirical, data-producing disempowerment study found. It classifies about 1.5M Claude.ai conversations from one week in December 2025. Reported rates: severe disempowerment potential in under 0.1% of conversations, milder potential in roughly 1 in 50–70, with higher rates in relationship and lifestyle topics. The potential reportedly rose over time and correlated with *higher* user satisfaction. These figures came from secondary write-ups; check them against the paper. The data is not public, so the site can cite the study but cannot update it.

## B. Economic and labor research


Acemoglu, D. & Johnson, S. (2023). **Power and Progress: Our Thousand-Year Struggle Over Technology and Prosperity.** PublicAffairs. [publisher](https://www.hachettebookgroup.com/titles/daron-acemoglu/power-and-progress/9781541702530/)\
**Type:** Book · **Used for:** background only\
A history of who captured the gains from new technologies, arguing that outcomes depended on countervailing power. The closest mainstream economics account to the site's focus on leverage rather than output.

Acemoglu, D. & Restrepo, P. (2019). **Automation and New Tasks: How Technology Displaces and Reinstates Labor.** *Journal of Economic Perspectives* 33(2). [AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jep.33.2.3)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** E1\
The displacement-versus-reinstatement framework that underlies most economics of automation and labor share.

Acemoglu, D. (2024). **The Simple Macroeconomics of AI.** MIT working paper. [PDF](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2024-04/The%20Simple%20Macroeconomics%20of%20AI.pdf)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
A well-known estimate that AI will add only modest productivity growth (about 0.7% total factor productivity over ten years). Listed as background; the research did not review it this session. Useful as a counterweight to high-growth forecasts.

Autor, D. (2024). **Applying AI to Rebuild Middle Class Jobs.** NBER Working Paper w32140. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32140)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
Argues that AI could be used to extend expert judgment to more workers, rebuilding middle-skill work, rather than only automating it. Relevant to the augmentation-vs-displacement distinction the site draws.

Autor, D., Chin, C., Salomons, A. & Seegmiller, B. (2024). **New Frontiers: The Origins and Content of New Work, 1940–2018.** *Quarterly Journal of Economics*. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjae008) ⚠ link not confirmed (HTTP 403; the site may block automated requests)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** E1, counter-evidence panel\
Shows that most of today's jobs did not exist in 1940. The strongest evidence for the counterargument that new kinds of work keep appearing.

Brookings Metro (2025). **Mapping the AI Economy** (ranking of 387 US metro areas, July 2025). No primary URL recorded; known through secondary coverage ([Dallas Innovates](https://dallasinnovates.com/?p=277606), Section G).\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
Ranks metro areas on AI talent, innovation and adoption. A static cross-section that could support geographic weighting. Bulk data availability was not confirmed.

Brynjolfsson, E., Chandar, B. & Chen, R. (2025; revised August 2026). **Canaries in the Coal Mine? Six Facts about the Recent Employment Effects of Artificial Intelligence.** Stanford Digital Economy Lab. [paper page](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publication/canaries-in-the-coal-mine-six-facts-about-the-recen) · [August 2026 update](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** method; background only (later version: young workers in AI-exposed jobs)\
Uses ADP payroll data (November 2022 to June 2026). Employment of 22–25-year-olds in highly AI-exposed occupations is about 19% below benchmark, up from 15–16% in the 2025 version. There is no comparable gap for experienced workers or economy-wide. The adjustment comes through less hiring, not layoffs, and is concentrated in occupations where AI automates rather than assists. The authors call it descriptive, not causal. The 19% is cumulative since late 2022, not a yearly rate, which matters for setting thresholds. Interest-rate rises hit the same sectors, so that confound applies.

Cazzaniga, M., et al. (2024). **Gen-AI: Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Work.** IMF Staff Discussion Note SDN/2024/001 (no URL recorded in project inputs).\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
Estimates that about 40% of global employment, and about 60% in advanced economies, is exposed to AI. Cited from background knowledge; not re-checked in the research.

Chandar, B. (2025). **AI and Labor Markets: What We Know and Don't Know.** Stanford Digital Economy Lab, 10 Oct 2025. [digitaleconomy.stanford.edu](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/ai-and-labor-markets-what-we-know-and-dont-know)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** method\
A review of the evidence. It notes that the Current Population Survey "has small sample sizes when filtering to specific age-occupation groups." CPS-based studies it covers (Gimbel et al. 2025 for the Yale Budget Lab/Brookings; Eckhardt & Goldschlag 2025; Dominski & Lee 2025) find at most small changes, and CPS evidence for ages 22–25 is "at best weak." This is why any free replication of the Canaries result must be labeled a rough proxy.

Cunningham, T. (2025). **Forecasts of AI Growth.** Personal blog, 19 Oct 2025. [tecunningham.github.io](https://tecunningham.github.io/posts/2025-10-19-forecasts-of-AI-growth.html)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** background only\
A survey of forecasts of AI's effect on economic growth. Useful context for how wide the range of expert expectations is.

Eloundou, T., Manning, S., Mishkin, P. & Rock, D. (2024). **GPTs are GPTs: Labor market impact potential of LLMs.** *Science* (2024); preprint 2023. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** method; background only (later version: exposure weights)\
Scores each occupation and task for exposure to large language models. Its data is the planned source of exposure weights for an open young-worker proxy and for exposure-weighted job postings. Exposure is technical potential, an upper bound; it does not separate automation from assistance. The data repo is listed in Section E.

Federal Reserve Board (2026). **Monitoring AI Adoption in the U.S. Economy.** FEDS Notes, 3 Apr 2026. [federalreserve.gov](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/monitoring-ai-adoption-in-the-u-s-economy-20260403.html)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** E2; method\
Compares measures of AI adoption. Census BTOS shows about 18% of firms using AI at end-2025, while the Atlanta Fed's survey shows about 78% of *employment* at adopting firms, a fourfold difference. It documents the BTOS wording change on 17 Nov 2025 and the 10–11% "don't know" rate. This is why E2 must always name its measure and annotate the break.

Felten, E., Raj, M. & Seamans, R. (2021). **Occupational, Industry, and Geographic Exposure to Artificial Intelligence: A Novel Dataset and Its Potential Uses.** *Strategic Management Journal*. [Wiley](https://sms.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/smj.3286) ⚠ link not confirmed (HTTP 403; the site may block automated requests)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** method; background only (later version: exposure weights)\
Introduces the AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE) index by occupation, industry and county. An alternative to Eloundou et al. for exposure weights. The data repo is in Section E.

Felten, E., Raj, M. & Seamans, R. (2023). **Occupational Heterogeneity in Exposure to Generative AI.** SSRN working paper 4414065. [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4414065) ⚠ link not confirmed (HTTP 403; the site may block automated requests)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
Updates the AIOE approach for generative AI (language modeling and image generation). The corresponding spreadsheets are in the AIOE repo.

Forecasting Research Institute (n.d.). **Forecasting the Economic Effects of AI.** [Substack](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/forecasting-the-economic-effects-of-ai)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
Structured expert and forecaster predictions about AI's economic effects. Background for thinking about plausible ranges; the site makes no predictions.

Goldin, C. & Katz, L. F. (2008). **The Race Between Education and Technology.** Harvard University Press (book).\
**Type:** Paper (book) · **Used for:** background only\
The classic account of how US wage inequality has followed the race between demand for skills and their supply through schooling. Useful historical framing for labor-market adjustment to technology. The paper's Sec. 6.2 also calls for historical analysis of past technological transitions.

Imas, A. & Trammell, P. (2026). **What remains scarce after AGI?** Dwarkesh Podcast, 4 Jun 2026. [dwarkesh.com](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/alex-imas-phil-trammell)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** E1, counter-evidence panel\
A conversation arguing that labor's share of income could stay roughly stable even after AGI. Relevant to how E1 is interpreted and to the counter-evidence panel.

Imas, A. (2026). **Has AI impacted the labor market yet?** *Ghosts of Electricity* (Substack), 29 Sep 2026. [Substack](https://aleximas.substack.com/p/has-ai-impacted-the-labor-market)\
**Type:** Blog or essay · **Used for:** E1, method\
An economist's review of the labor-market evidence on AI. Imas's broader "relational sector" argument (work and spending shift to what stays scarce) is a rival reading for E1: a stable labor share does not by itself show that workers' leverage is intact. Figures should be checked against the studies it cites before use.

Indeed Hiring Lab (2026). **Post on the AI share of US job postings at end-2025** (January 2026; title not recorded). [hiringlab.org](https://www.hiringlab.org/?p=20272)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only (later version: job postings)\
Reports that 4.2% of US postings mentioned AI or generative AI at end-2025, with AI postings 134% above February 2020 against 6% for all postings. Mentions of AI measure demand for AI skills, not replacement of workers.

International Labour Organization (2025). **Generative AI and Jobs: A Refined Global Index of Occupational Exposure.** ILO Working Paper 140, May 2025, DOI 10.54394/HETP0387, CC BY 4.0. [ILO page](https://www.ilo.org/node/706766) · [PDF mirror](https://www.developmentaid.org/api/frontend/cms/file/2025/05/WP140_web.pdf)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
Exposure scores for each 4-digit ISCO occupation, built from about 30,000 tasks and a worker survey. One in four workers worldwide is in an occupation with some exposure; 3.3% are in the highest category (4.7% of women, 2.4% of men). The ILO describes the scores as upper-limit scenarios. Read via search summary.

Karabarbounis, L. & Neiman, B. (2014). **The Global Decline of the Labor Share.** *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 129(1): 61–103. [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article/129/1/61/1899422) ⚠ link not confirmed (HTTP 403; the site may block automated requests)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** E1 (context)\
Shows that labor's share of income fell in most countries from the 1980s, and links the decline to falling prices of investment goods. Essential background for E1: the decline predates generative AI by decades, which is why E1 has a *weak* attribution badge and is compared against its 2010–2019 trend.

Korinek, A. & Suh, D. (2024). **Scenarios for the Transition to AGI.** NBER Working Paper w32255. [NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32255)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** background only\
Models when wages rise or collapse as automation spreads. Economic grounding for the paper's economic chapter and for how E1 and E4 might move.

MIT Media Lab & Oak Ridge National Laboratory (Chopra, Raskar et al.) (2025). **Iceberg Index.** [project overview](https://www.media.mit.edu/projects/iceberg/overview) · [report PDF](https://iceberg.mit.edu/report.pdf)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
Simulates 151M US workers and estimates that 11.7% of US wage value (about $1.2T a year) is in skills current AI could technically perform, against about 2.2% covered by visible adoption. MIT says it measures technical exposure, not displacement. Read via search summary; bulk data was not confirmed.

Muro, M., Methkupally, S. & Kinder, M. (2025). **Generative AI exposure by US county** (Brookings Metro). No primary URL recorded; known through [GlobeSt coverage](https://globest.com/2025/03/04/generative-ai-impact-on-workforce-varies-widely-by-region) (Section G).\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
County-level maps of generative-AI exposure built on the OpenAI (Eloundou et al.) scores. A static cross-section for geographic weighting.

Stanford HAI (2026). **AI Index Report 2026** (9th edition, April 2026). [hai.stanford.edu](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** counter-evidence panel; background only\
The annual reference on AI trends. The Economy chapter reports US private AI investment of about $285.9B in 2025 and global generative-AI adoption of 53%. The Policy chapter covers AI legislation, but the research could not retrieve the 2026 legislative counts. The report itself notes that private-investment figures understate China. Its chart data is listed in Section E.

World Economic Forum (2025). **Future of Jobs Report 2025.** [WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/) ⚠ link not confirmed (HTTP 403; site may block automated requests)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
A survey of large employers on expected job and skill changes to 2030. Employer expectations, not measured outcomes.

## C. Government and democracy research


Bates, R. & Lien, D. (1985). **A Note on Taxation, Development, and Representative Government.** *Politics & Society* 14(1). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/003232928501400102) ⚠ link not confirmed (HTTP 403; the site may block automated requests)\
**Type:** Paper · **Used for:** G1\
A short classic on why governments that depend on taxing mobile wealth must offer representation in return. Part of the theory behind G1.

Carnegie Endowment for International Peace (Feldstein, S.) (2019). **The Global Expansion of AI Surveillance.** [carnegieendowment.org](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2019/09/the-global-expansion-of-ai-surveillance)\
**Type:** Report · **Used for:** background only\
Introduced the AI Global Surveillance Index (176 countries, at least 75 using AI surveillance). A 2022 revision exists (dataset in Section E). No update since 2022 was found, so it can only serve as a static baseline.
